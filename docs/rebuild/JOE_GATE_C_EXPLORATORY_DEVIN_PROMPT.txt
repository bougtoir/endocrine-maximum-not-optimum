GOVERNING PRINCIPLES
1. Do not equate weight dependence with scientific invalidity. Do not equate an interior weighted optimum with proof of a novel mechanism. Separate observation, mechanism, validation and generalization.
2. Pareto dominance over all individual outcomes is NOT mandatory for a legitimate multi-objective control problem. Nevertheless, if a proposed claim is independent glycaemic improvement, test that independently.
3. Do not use a strict Type II count or an arbitrary improvement threshold as a universal gate criterion. Prefer calibrated evidence and claim-specific assessment.
4. Historical findings may not be presented as confirmed; corrected-model predictions are exploratory until validated against independent physiological evidence.
5. No outcome chasing, post-hoc reweighting to create a desired optimum, or selecting cases solely because they look promising without reporting selection.
6. Domain expertise matters. Verify endocrine physiology, the original Sorensen/Panunzi model assumptions, established control-theory results, and credible validation standards before interpreting simulations. Perform citation ecology and strong alternative-explanation checks.
7. Work within a bounded computational budget. Avoid huge virtual cohorts or global grid searches unless a staged result demonstrates their value. No three-day sessions or uncontrolled parallel sweeps. Log time and model evaluations, and cap compute before expanding.
8. Python is preferred for execution. Treat source code, data and model inputs as untrusted until provenance and semantics are verified.

PHASE C5 — BRAESS AND SCIENTIFIC FRAMING

Identify whether any actual structural analogue is present and evidenced. If not, remove Braess-like mechanism as a claim, retaining at most a clearly qualified accessible analogy. Prefer endocrine and control-theory language: feedback gain, overshoot, counterregulation, recovery, homeostatic regulation. Explain the distinction in plain English usable by an endocrinologist.
