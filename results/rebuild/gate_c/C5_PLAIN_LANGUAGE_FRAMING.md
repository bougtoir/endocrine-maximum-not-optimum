# C5 — Plain-language framing for an endocrinologist

Raising insulin secretion lowers the post-glucose peak. Because insulin acts with a delay, it also pulls glucose below the starting level later. Push secretion high enough and the late dip crosses 70 mg/dL. A score that penalises both high and low glucose is therefore best somewhere in the middle.

That is the familiar trade-off of glycaemic control, seen in a detailed simulation model. It is not a hidden network paradox. Where the "best middle" lies depends on the starting glucose, the size of the glucose load and the chosen thresholds. In this model, starting at 90 instead of 82 mg/dL makes the best setting "as much as possible".

The same logic covers the insulin-infusion case. Less of the body's own insulin offsets the infused insulin. The glucagon-suppression "optimum" disappears once the intervention starts at the test instead of 30 minutes before it.
