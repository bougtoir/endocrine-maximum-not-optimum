# C5 — Braess claim boundary

**What Braess's paradox is.** In a network where flow distributes across routes by selfish equilibrium, adding capacity or a route can worsen total performance. Three ingredients are needed:
1. alternative routes;
2. decentralised equilibrium routing;
3. a capacity or route addition that changes the equilibrium.

**What the corrected model shows.** In every case, the intervention scales a single flux in a closed feedback loop: secretion or action. There are no alternative routes, and no equilibrium among agents choosing between routes.

- The interior optimum in (a) comes from a delayed feedback undershoot.
- The interior optimum in (b) comes from exogenous and endogenous insulin balancing against fixed thresholds.
- The interior optimum in (c) is a protocol artifact.

None of them is structurally analogous to Braess's paradox.

**Decision.** Remove "Braess-like mechanism" as a claim. If the term appears at all, it should be one sentence of analogy only, together with an explicit statement that the mechanism is not Braess's paradox. Recommended language: feedback gain, delay, undershoot, counterregulation, glycaemic risk trade-off.
